What the picture shows

  • Your VTXO is a coin with an orange ring, sitting on the Ark line. Your VTXO is a leaf of the round's tree, so the exit has to publish the tree's transactions that lead to it. In a 64-leaf round (radix 4) that path is 4 transactions: the root, two branches, then your leaf. The round's own transaction is already on-chain.
  • Lift off. The coin rises and the Ark server greys out. None of this needs the server.
  • The path unrolls in orange, as onboarding drew your branch, down to the round's block on the Bitcoin row. The faint stubs are everyone else's branches. We draw only yours.
  • One transaction per block. The bottom transaction drops into the blinking "next" block. Its fee rides on its anchor: a dot flies in from your on-chain wallet, whose balance ticks down. The block is mined (real heights), the row slides, and the branch steps down. A transaction is only sent once the one before it is in a block, because its fee transaction can only spend confirmed coins.
  • Your coin lands in a block. Then the timelock: 144 blocks, about a day, so Ark can stop a VTXO that was already spent. The lock opens: "Ready to claim".
  • Claim: one more transaction, with its fee taken from the amount, then "Claimed in block N" and the finale.

Wide and foldable

  • iPhone Duo and Android fold open: the picture and one line of explanation lead; the amount, rows and actions trail. Sheets and dialogs stay on the trailing side. Nothing crosses the fold.
